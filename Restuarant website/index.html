The History of Charcoal-Grilled Chicken

Long before it had a name, this was just fire and chicken. Here's how it became the dish we grill today.

1Ancient Fire Cooking

Grilling over open coals is one of the oldest cooking methods there is — going back to when humans first learned to control fire. The smoky char quickly stopped being a side effect and became the whole point: people started cooking specifically to chase that flavor.

2The Levantine Charcoal-Chicken Tradition

In Lebanon and across the Levant, marinating whole chickens in garlic, lemon and spice before slow-turning them over charcoal became a street-food staple by the mid-20th century. Rotisserie stalls — "farrouj mshawi" — turned into neighborhood fixtures, the smell alone enough to pull people off the sidewalk.

3Why Charcoal, Not Gas

Charcoal burns hotter and drier than gas, crisping the skin while the smoke perfumes the meat all the way through. That's the detail that separates plain "grilled chicken" from real charcoal-grilled chicken — and why we've never swapped our grills for anything else.

4A Dish That Travelled

From roadside grills to family tables, charcoal chicken became a shared comfort food — always served with garlic sauce, chili sauce, pickles and fries. That's the exact lineup we still serve at Restaurant Makki today.
